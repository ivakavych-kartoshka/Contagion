We thank Reviewer 1 for noting our per-hop framing, statistical restraint, and content-form ablation. We ran three targeted follow-up analyses to address W1, W2, and Q1 (to be included in the camera-ready version).

**1. Propagation vs. adoption (W1).** Auditing our existing frontier logs confirms Family B is strictly level-(a): adoption is 0/30 on Llama [0.000, 0.114] (151/151 downstream positives are quotes) and 0/20 on DeepSeek [0.000, 0.161]. To test level-(b) directly, we ran a new deterministic decision-hijack task (Family C, flipping DECISION: HIGH->LOW; n=40 natural, 30/edge fresh, clean baseline 1.000 [0.839, 1.000], 0% parse fail, hand-verified). Level-(b) adoption propagates strongly end-to-end: 0.975 [0.871, 0.996] on Llama and 0.625 [0.470, 0.758] on DeepSeek, even though literal text (a) drops to 0.025 and 0.000. Multi-hop propagation thus holds for real behavioural hijacks, and (a)/(b) can decouple in either direction.

**2. **3. Why Llama a1->a2 differs (Q1).** Llama a0->a1 saturates at 1.000 (Table 4). At a1->a2 (reviewer), role permutation shows Llama tracks role labels (mean |Delta|=0.039 vs. 0.166 by position, App. O Table 13) with strong context sensitivity (chi^2=13.40, p=0.0014, Sec. 5.5). By contrast, our new DeepSeek replay (n=40/30) shows its reviewer passes canonical text (a1->a2: 0.933 vs. 0.800 in-context, s_nat=0.846, p=0.254), shifting DeepSeek's form gap to a0->a1 (+0.433). On Llama a2->a3, replay failed self-check (0.733 vs. 0.947, Table 4; App. G), so no reversal is claimed.
